\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experiment Setup}
\label{sec:experiment_setup}

All experiments draw from \num{10000} globally distributed populated-place requests. Scheduling uses a \SI{24}{\hour} window, circular \SI{400}{\kilo\metre} orbit, and \tabref{tab:lookahead_cases} instruments. Lookaheads are evaluated at imaging time, tying cloud state and insertion feasibility to acquisition epoch. Unless noted, the BCM prior is $p=0.34$.

We compare four levels of information use. The \emph{conventional} policy executes the ground-planned schedule under realized cloud truth, while the \emph{omniscient} MILP re-optimizes after every access state is revealed and serves as an upper reference. The \emph{greedy} lookahead policy selects the pitch revealing the largest number of unobserved accesses and looks whenever a nonempty view exists, without a schedule-value gate. The break-even and two-anchor policies look only when their respective value estimates are positive.

For policy $\pi$, we report the realized conventional-to-omniscient gap fraction
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{closed}}(\pi)
=
\frac{J_{\pi}-J_{\mathrm{conv}}}
{J_{\mathrm{omni}}-J_{\mathrm{conv}}},
\label{eq:gap_fraction_closed}
\end{equation}
alongside absolute clear-image utility. Lookahead use is the number of lookahead actions divided by the number of accesses in the initial conventional schedule.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Heuristic Calibration}
\label{sec:exp_heuristic_calibration}

We calibrate two-anchor advantage $\widehat{K}_{2a}(g_{\mathrm{phys}})-B$ and its break-even surrogate $\widehat{K}_{b}(g_{\mathrm{phys}})-B$ against paired Monte Carlo local insertion advantage in sampled IID repair windows. The corresponding $g_{\mathrm{break}}$ and margin $M=g_{\mathrm{break}}-g_{\mathrm{phys}}$ remain gate diagnostics rather than estimates of advantage itself.

The sampler varies candidate density, cloud probability, physical spacing, and missed-task burden without conditioning on the sign of either heuristic. Each Monte Carlo value averages IID Bernoulli cloud states at fixed access times rather than running a full orbit-and-weather simulation. Thus this experiment isolates the exchangeable-access model; Experiments~2--3 add real geometry, pitch-dependent support, and schedule repair. Because $B=p(N_{\mathrm{sched}}+N_{\mathrm{missed}})$ is unbiased here, residual bias reflects the chain approximation.

We measure continuous agreement using the concordance correlation coefficient (CCC), which penalizes both dispersion and systematic offset from the one-to-one line. The two-anchor estimate attains CCC $0.992$ with bias $-0.025$, versus CCC $0.982$ and bias $-0.305$ for break-even.
Across 400 windows, two-anchor has \SI{97.3}{\percent} sign agreement, \SI{2.1}{\percent} false accepts among acceptances, and \SI{3.6}{\percent} false rejects among positive windows. Break-even has \SI{92.3}{\percent}, \SI{1.8}{\percent}, and \SI{14.4}{\percent}, respectively, confirming its conservatism. Against an oracle that accepts positive Monte Carlo advantage, mean regret is 0.005 versus 0.022 clear-image equivalents per window. \SI{95}{\percent} bootstrap intervals resample windows, propagate pointwise Monte Carlo error, and bound CCC, bias, and the least-squares line.

\subsection{Experiment 2: IID End-to-End Scheduling}
\label{sec:exp_iid_scheduling}

We test greedy, break-even, and two-anchor policies under IID Bernoulli truth. Each trial builds the conventional schedule, samples states with $p_{\mathrm{clear}}=0.34$, computes the omniscient bound, and replays policies on identical accesses and truth. Dates and RAAN phase match the BCM study; only cloud generation changes (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_bcm_provenance}). Across \num{12} paired \SI{24}{\hour} trials, we sweep $\Theta_h=\Theta_v\in\{\ang{45},\ang{60}\}$ and $t_{\mathrm{slew}}(\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}})\in\{25,35,45\}$~s, scoring \num{64} pitches per gap.
Across the sweep, greedy closes \SI{58.1}{\percent} of the conventional-to-omniscient gap with 0.269 lookaheads per scheduled access. Break-even closes \SI{78.2}{\percent} with 0.162 and two-anchor \SI{79.0}{\percent} with 0.166: higher closure with about \SI{40}{\percent} fewer actions. FoV dominates sensitivity. Enlarging it from \ang{45}$\times$\ang{45} to \ang{60}$\times$\ang{60} improves every agility cell; break-even gains \num{5.1}--\num{16.5} percentage points from \SI{25}{\second} to \SI{45}{\second}, with two-anchor similar. Greedy degrades with slew burden, while value gates avoid much of this loss by selecting fewer high-margin actions.
At \ang{60}$\times$\ang{60}, break-even/two-anchor close \SI{80.6}{\percent}/\SI{80.9}{\percent} at \SI{25}{\second} and peak at \SI{85.7}{\percent}/\SI{85.4}{\percent} at \SI{45}{\second}, while use rises only from about 0.16 to 0.20. These normalized fractions do not rank absolute utility: for \ang{60}$\times$\ang{60} break-even, mean clear images fall from 152.2 to 125.7 across those agility settings. Thus the policies schedule nearly identically; two-anchor estimates finite-window value more accurately, while break-even yields a dead-time threshold. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_iid_scheduling_summary} gives all cells.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Realistic BCM Case Study}
\label{sec:exp_bcm_case_study}

We repeat the sweep using geostationary Binary Cloud Mask truth in \num{12} paired \SI{24}{\hour} windows. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_bcm_provenance} gives trial starts and product identifiers. RAAN phase matching preserves the Earth-fixed ground track across dates; seasonal variation comes from illumination and clouds. Policies retain $p_{\mathrm{clear}}=0.34$, while truth uses the nearest product at each imaging time. No correlation model is fit: structure enters only through sampled masks. Paired policies share requests, accesses, conventional schedule, and truth.

Under BCM truth (\figref{fig:bcm_scheduling_tradeoff}), break-even closes \SI{77.6}{\percent} using 0.161 lookaheads per scheduled access, and two-anchor closes \SI{79.0}{\percent} using 0.165. Greedy closes \SI{43.6}{\percent} using 0.264. Relative to IID, break-even changes from \SI{78.2}{\percent} to \SI{77.6}{\percent} and two-anchor remains \SI{79.0}{\percent}, whereas greedy drops from \SI{58.1}{\percent} to \SI{43.6}{\percent}. With dates, requests, geometry, and settings matched, scalar-prior screening therefore remains effective despite BCM correlation and coherent boundaries.
The best normalized cell is \ang{60}$\times$\ang{60} at \SI{45}{\second}: break-even closes \SI{85.6}{\percent} with 0.196 lookaheads per scheduled access, and two-anchor \SI{86.0}{\percent} with 0.199. The small difference agrees with Experiment~1's bias result. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_bcm_scheduling_summary} reports absolute and normalized results; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_bcm_provenance} reports timings.

\subsection{Experiment 4: Overflight Case Study}
\label{sec:exp_overflight_case_study}

The full-day sweeps hide within-pass behavior, so \figref{fig:brazil_overflight_case_study} replays both gates on one access set, conventional schedule, camera model, and IID realization. The \SI{30}{\minute} South America pass has \num{266} candidates and begins offshore; a zero-utility virtual anchor \SI{2}{\minute} after its start exposes the initial reconnaissance gap before the first conventional image. Conventional execution schedules \num{36} real tasks and collects \num{7} clear images; omniscient scheduling collects \num{27}. Both policies take \num{5} lookaheads, repair to \num{29} tasks, and collect \num{25} clear images.

The pass also exposes scalar pitch search's geometric failure mode. A high-value access can enter the rectangular FoV laterally rather than through the modeled forward edge, yet projection includes it in the one-dimensional forward window. The gates still attain identical utility on different score scales.
