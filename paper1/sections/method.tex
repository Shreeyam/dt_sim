\section{Systems Analysis}
\label{sec:systems_analysis}

The onboard algorithm is derived from the flight-system constraints.
Rather than solving an adaptive stochastic scheduling problem with continuous lookahead times, continuous pointing angles, unknown cloud states, and a combinatorial imaging schedule, we first use scheduling structure, spacecraft geometry, and agility limits to collapse dynamic tasking into local decisions made in schedule gaps.
For each gap, the spacecraft asks whether one lookahead maneuver is physically meaningful and, if so, which pitch angles are feasible.
The lookahead decision method then estimates whether any feasible maneuver has positive schedule-level value.

\subsection{Baseline Scheduling Model}
\label{sec:scheduling}

Orbital parameters map requests in $\mathcal{R}$ to accesses $a_i=(r_i,t_i,\theta_i)$, recording request, overpass time, and off-nadir roll~\citep{eddy_task_2021}. Point, strip, and area requests are represented as points after standard tessellation or decomposition~\citep{eddy_task_2021, xu_multi-satellite_2020}.
The scheduling problem is to select a subset $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ maximizing total utility subject to agility constraints:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} \sum_{a_i \in \boldsymbol{\sigma}} u_i, \quad
\text{s.t.} \quad t_j - t_i \geq t_{\mathrm{slew}}(\theta_j - \theta_i) \;\;\forall\; a_i, a_j \in \boldsymbol{\sigma},\; t_j > t_i,
\label{eq:scheduling}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\mathrm{slew}}(\Delta\theta)=t_s+\beta\sqrt{|\Delta\theta|}$ is a bang-bang model with settling time $t_s$ and rate parameter $\beta$. A mixed-integer linear program (MILP) solves~\eqref{eq:scheduling} with an added constraint preventing repeated collection of a request.

\subsection{Dynamic Tasking Formulation}
\label{sec:dt_formulation}

We modulate each intrinsic access value $c_i$ by cloud state:
\begin{equation}
u_i = c_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{clear}(r_i, t_i)\}}.
\end{equation}
We study unit utility, $c_i=1$~\citep{kacker_spacecraft_2025}. A \emph{lookahead observation} reveals cloud states unknown during ground planning, after which the schedule is repaired with updated utilities.

Lookahead utility is the \emph{advantage} of revealed, insertable clear accesses minus the \emph{opportunity cost} of slew time, reducing the problem to local decision analysis.

We analyze the body-fixed onboard-lookahead case because it is tightly coupled to spacecraft agility: the same bus that images targets must also slew to acquire information about them.
The field of view, horizon lead time, and slew time therefore directly determine the feasible onboard decision space.
This coupling is weaker for sensor webs, leader-follower constellations, external meteorology, radar, and steerable instruments, which instead shift the trade toward communication latency, sensor cadence, and data-fusion assumptions~\citep{chien_autonomous_2005, chien_automated_2020, chien_using_2019, kacker_leveraging_2025, mahonchak_goes-r_nodate}.


\subsection{Lookahead Geometry and Agility Constraints}
\label{sec:lookahead_design}

For sizing, the lower vertical detector edge points toward nadir at zero body roll and pitch, so the boresight pitch is $b=\Theta_v/2$ for full vertical FoV $\Theta_v$.
We require the lookahead footprint to intersect each lateral boundary of the primary-imager field of regard at an actionable point. This is an edge-contact requirement: each boundary must be reached somewhere with enough lead time for the subsequent maneuver.

\paragraph{Lead time and vertical FoV.}
For a circular orbit with $a=R_E+h$ and $n=\sqrt{\mu/a^3}$, a visible point at signed along-track central angle $\psi$ has lead time $t_{\mathrm{lead}}=\psi/n$. The horizon bounds this lead by
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{horizon}} = \frac{1}{n} \arccos\!\left(\frac{R_E}{a}\right),
\label{eq:horizon}
\end{equation}
which is approximately \SI{305}{\second} at \SI{400}{\kilo\metre} altitude. At zero body roll and pitch, the upper vertical detector edge points forward by $\Theta_v$; increasing $\Theta_v$ therefore extends the available lead, up to the limb (\figref{fig:lookahead_time_constraints}). A contact is actionable only when $t_{\mathrm{lead}}\geq t_{\mathrm{req}}$, where $t_{\mathrm{req}}$ includes the applicable repointing, settling, acquisition, and inference times. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_fov_projection} gives the spherical ray intersection and detector projection.

\paragraph{Detector bounds.}
Let $\delta$, $\eta$, and $d$ be a target's horizontal offset, vertical offset, and depth in the lookahead camera frame. Normalized detector coordinates are $u=\delta/d$ and $v=\eta/d$. For $d>0$, the rectangular detector contains the target when
\begin{equation}
|u|\leq\tan\frac{\Theta_h}{2},
\qquad
|v|\leq\tan\frac{\Theta_v}{2},
\label{eq:detector_bounds}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta_h$ is full horizontal FoV. Thus horizontal sizing asks how wide the detector must be to reach both projected FoR boundaries (\figref{fig:camera_fov_limit_boxes}).

\paragraph{Horizontal sizing.}
For body roll $\varphi$, let $\mathcal{D}_\sigma(\varphi;t_{\mathrm{req}})$ contain the Earth-visible points on FoR edge $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$ that are front-facing, satisfy the vertical bound in~\eqref{eq:detector_bounds}, and have sufficient lead time. Parameterizing each edge by along-track position $x$, the required horizontal FoV is
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{h,\min}(\varphi;t_{\mathrm{req}})
=
2\arctan\!\left[
\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}}
\;\inf_{x\in\mathcal{D}_\sigma(\varphi;t_{\mathrm{req}})}
|u_\sigma(x;\varphi)|
\right].
\label{eq:horizontal_fov_requirement}
\end{equation}
For each edge, we find the contact requiring the smallest width, then size for the harder edge. If either admissible set is empty, that attitude is infeasible. We evaluate nadir ($\varphi=0$) and maximum roll ($\varphi=\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}$) separately; an instrument required to support both takes the larger width. These are two design attitudes, rather than a guarantee over intermediate rolls.

\paragraph{Flat-Earth sizing rule.}
At nadir, a flat-Earth approximation makes the trade explicit. A primary-imager FoR half-angle $\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}$ places the lateral boundaries at $y=\pm h\tan\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}$. The upper detector edge reaches $x=h\tan\Theta_v$, with camera depth $d=x\sin b+h\cos b$. Substitution in~\eqref{eq:detector_bounds} gives
\begin{equation}
\tan\frac{\Theta_{h,\min}^{(N)}}{2}
\approx
\frac{h\tan\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}}
{h\tan\Theta_v\sin b+h\cos b}
=
\frac{\tan\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}\cos\Theta_v}
{\cos(\Theta_v/2)},
\qquad 0<\Theta_v<\ang{90}.
\label{eq:flat_horizontal_sizing}
\end{equation}
The rule applies when this contact has sufficient lead time. A larger vertical FoV reaches farther ahead, where the lateral boundaries occupy less horizontal detector width. For $\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}}=\ang{45}$, vertical FoVs of \ang{45} and \ang{60} require approximately \ang{74.9} and \ang{60} horizontal FoV; spherical projection at \SI{400}{\kilo\metre} gives \ang{73.0} and \ang{54.4}. Near the limb, especially at large roll, we use the spherical calculation in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_fov_evaluation}.

\subsection{Agility Limits}
\label{sec:agility_limits}

The same systems constraints bracket any dynamic-tasking policy.
With strictly positive lead and vanishing maneuver, acquisition, and inference time, full advance revelation recovers the omniscient MILP.
Feasible lookahead sets shrink monotonically with slew time; once no schedule gap admits a round trip, dynamic tasking reduces to conventional execution.

This systems analysis reduces the onboard policy to one candidate lookahead action per schedule gap, searched over a discrete grid of feasible pitch angles.
The remaining question is how to value each candidate angle.

\section{Lookahead Decision Method}
\label{sec:method}

We approximate schedule repair inside a bounded lookahead window using the following four assumptions:
\begin{align}
&\textbf{(A1)}\quad \tau_{ij} \approx g \;\;\forall\,(i,j)\ \text{inside the local repair window}, && \text{constant effective spacing;} \notag\\
&\textbf{(A2)}\quad t_i \overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{U}(T_1, T_2), && \text{exchangeable access times;} \notag\\
&\textbf{(A3)}\quad X_i \overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bern}(p), && \text{i.i.d.\ cloud states;} \notag\\
&\textbf{(A4)}\quad c_i \approx \bar{c}, && \text{locally equal intrinsic utility.}
\label{eq:assumptions}
\end{align}
(A1) compresses pairwise slews into an effective dead time; (A2) equates fixed-count uniform times with Poisson arrivals conditioned on their count. (A3)--(A4) express value in expected clear-image equivalents; the repair MILP can still use heterogeneous priorities after acceptance.

\subsection{Two-Anchor Lookahead Value Model}
\label{sec:heuristic}

At pitch $\alpha$ relative to rest angle $\alpha_0$, camera projection gives the unobserved, visible accesses no earlier than the next scheduled image: $\mathcal{V}_\alpha$, with $N_{\mathrm{obs}}(\alpha)=|\mathcal{V}_\alpha|$. Before scoring pitches, each gap fixes $t_L$ as the previous scheduled image (or window start) and $t_R$ as the first scheduled image after the largest feasible detector support.
Let
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{anc}} = t_R - t_L
\label{eq:anchor_span}
\end{equation}
be the common anchor interval, translated to $[0,L_{\mathrm{anc}}]$ for every pitch. The detector need not span it.
For pitch $\alpha$, let
\begin{equation}
0 \leq \ell_\alpha < r_\alpha \leq L_{\mathrm{anc}},
\qquad
W_\alpha = r_\alpha-\ell_\alpha
\label{eq:detector_support}
\end{equation}
be detector support after latest-feasible timing, clipped near the next scheduled image and far at the horizon. Let $N_{\mathrm{sched}}$ count scheduled tasks in $(t_L,t_R)$ and $N_{\mathrm{missed}}$ those forfeited during slew.
The net lookahead utility is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta J} = \underbrace{\hat{J}^+(\mathcal{V}_\alpha)}_{\text{advantage}} - \underbrace{\hat{J}^-(\mathcal{V}_\alpha)}_{\text{opportunity cost}}.
\label{eq:net_advantage}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Opportunity cost.}
Each forfeited task has expected clear utility $p\bar{c}$, so
\begin{equation}
\hat{J}^- = p \cdot \bar{c} \cdot N_{\mathrm{missed}},
\label{eq:opp_cost}
\end{equation}
where $p=P(\text{clear})$ and $\bar{c}$ is mean intrinsic utility. The timing rule in \secref{sec:timing} normally makes $N_{\mathrm{missed}}=0$; we retain it for calibration and alternative timing.

\paragraph{Poissonized chain model.}
An observed clear access has value only if insertable between its scheduled anchors. Within the revealed support, prior thinning gives clear-candidate density
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha) = \frac{pN_{\mathrm{obs}}(\alpha)}{W_\alpha}.
\label{eq:clear_density}
\end{equation}
The stochastic approximation exploits a standard symmetry: conditional on observing $N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ arrivals in an interval, the unordered times of a homogeneous Poisson process are i.i.d.\ uniform~\citep{feller_introduction_1991,david_order_2004}.
We therefore represent the fixed visible candidates by a process conditioned on its count, then approximate the unknown clear subset by an unconditioned thinned process of rate $\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}$.
Compressing pairwise slews into dead time $g$ turns local repair into a one-dimensional packing problem.
In body-fixed experiments, $g_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is the conservative worst-case slew across the primary-imager FoR; tighter local or angle-dependent bounds can replace it.
With the left and right schedule anchors translated to $0$ and $L_{\mathrm{anc}}$, an insertable clear chain must satisfy
\begin{equation}
t_1 \geq g,\qquad
t_{i+1}-t_i \geq g,\qquad
L_{\mathrm{anc}}-t_k \geq g.
\label{eq:anchored_chain_constraints}
\end{equation}
The anchors remove support lying within $g$ of either boundary. Define
\begin{equation}
c_L(\alpha,g)=\max(0,\;g-\ell_\alpha),\qquad
c_R(\alpha,g)=\max(0,\;g-(L_{\mathrm{anc}}-r_\alpha)),
\label{eq:support_clearances}
\end{equation}
and the usable support length
\begin{equation}
U_\alpha(g)=W_\alpha-c_L(\alpha,g)-c_R(\alpha,g).
\label{eq:usable_support}
\end{equation}
After accepting a chain member, the next requires clearance $g$ plus an exponential wait of mean $1/\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}$~\citep{feller_probability_1948,pyke_renewal_1958}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[R] = g + \frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}},
\label{eq:renewal_spacing}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is repaired-chain spacing.

For finite support $[\ell_\alpha,r_\alpha]$, the probability of selecting at least $k$ internal points with gaps at least $g$ is
\begin{equation}
G_\alpha(k;\,g) =
\frac{\gamma\!\big(k,\;\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(U_\alpha(g) - (k{-}1)g\bigr)\big)}{\Gamma(k)},
\qquad U_\alpha(g) \geq (k{-}1)g,
\label{eq:chain_prob}
\end{equation}
with $G_\alpha(k;g)=0$ when $U_\alpha(g) < (k{-}1)g$.
The corresponding expected number of insertable internal points is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g)
=
\sum_{k\geq 1}
G_\alpha(k;\,g).
\label{eq:chain_expectation}
\end{equation}
For full support, $c_L=c_R=g$ and~\eqref{eq:chain_prob} has residual length $L_{\mathrm{anc}}-(k{+}1)g$. The finite-window advantage is
\begin{equation}
\hat{J}^+_{2a}
=
\bar{c}\,\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g),
\label{eq:renewal_advantage}
\end{equation}
The onboard gate can evaluate $\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g_{\mathrm{phys}})$ directly; the sum is finite, monotone in $g$, and cheap for short windows.

\paragraph{Break-even approximation.}
For a closed-form threshold, we approximate the finite-window chain by its long-run renewal rate on the same usable detector support:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{K}_{b}(\alpha,g)
\;\approx\;
\frac{U_\alpha(g)}{g + 1/\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha)}
\;=\;
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha)U_\alpha(g)}
{1 + \lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha) g}.
\label{eq:chain_rational_approx}
\end{equation}
This average packing rate increases with candidate density and decreases monotonically with dead time, enabling an algebraic threshold. We use the finite two-anchor form as the accurate estimator and $\widehat{K}_{b}$ as the break-even approximation.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_renewal} derives~\eqref{eq:chain_prob} and discusses the residual bias.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=.8\linewidth]{figures/chain_abstraction.pdf}
    \caption{Two-anchor value window. Scheduled anchors bound local repair; gray regions are not actionable. A lookahead must add more expected insertable utility than the local replacement burden.}
    \label{fig:chain_abstraction}
\end{figure}

Now write the net advantage in units of average task utility:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\widehat{\Delta J}_{2a}}{\bar{c}}
=
\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g)
-
\left(p\,N_{\mathrm{sched}} + \frac{\hat{J}^-}{\bar{c}}\right).
\label{eq:normalised_advantage}
\end{equation}
We define
\begin{equation}
B \;=\; p\,N_{\mathrm{sched}} + \frac{\hat{J}^-}{\bar{c}},
\label{eq:break_even_baseline}
\end{equation}
as the local replacement burden measured in clear-image equivalents.
The first term is the expected utility already present in the scheduled anchor interval and therefore represents what the repaired schedule must preserve.
The second converts maneuver-induced loss into the same units.
Thus $B$ is not a cloud or geometry parameter by itself; it is the number of insertable clear-image equivalents a lookahead must produce before it creates net schedule value.
Fixing $B$ across each pitch grid avoids boundary discontinuities. It is conservative for partial detector support because repair may retain scheduled tasks outside that support. The finite-window gate is $\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g_{\mathrm{phys}})>B$; the engineering threshold solves $\widehat{K}_{b}(\alpha,g_{\mathrm{break}})=B$.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{break}}(\alpha)
=
\sup\left\{
g \geq 0:
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha)U_\alpha(g)}
{1+\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha)g}
> B
\right\}.
\label{eq:g_break_alpha}
\end{equation}
If $\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}(\alpha)U_\alpha(0)\leq B$, even zero dead time cannot beat the baseline. For full anchor support,~\eqref{eq:g_break_alpha} becomes
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{break}}
\;\approx\;
\frac{L_{\mathrm{anc}}\left(pN_{\mathrm{obs}} - B\right)}
{pN_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(2+B\right)},
\qquad pN_{\mathrm{obs}} > B.
\label{eq:g_break_full_support}
\end{equation}
Monotonicity in $g$ makes the gate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta J} > 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g_{\mathrm{phys}} < g_{\mathrm{break}},
\label{eq:break_even_gate}
\end{equation}
up to the approximation in~\eqref{eq:chain_rational_approx}.

$g_{\mathrm{break}}$ is not a replacement for the spacecraft slew time.
It is the maximum effective dead time that the current observation window can tolerate before the expected value of looking falls to zero.
The policy therefore compares this schedule-derived threshold with the physical dead time $g_{\mathrm{phys}}$; with zero opportunity cost, the threshold increases with usable support and decreases with existing schedule utility.

Because projection and timing change observed sets, support, and clearances, each pitch has margin
\begin{equation}
M(\alpha)
= g_{\mathrm{break}}(\alpha) - g_{\mathrm{phys}}(\alpha),
\qquad
\alpha^\dagger = \arg\max_{\alpha \in \mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{feas}}} M(\alpha),
\label{eq:break_even_margin}
\end{equation}
and acceptance requires $M(\alpha^\dagger)>0$. The two-anchor policy instead maximizes $\widehat{K}_{2a}(\alpha,g_{\mathrm{phys}})-B$ on the same support and baseline. Body-fixed scoring uses $g_{\mathrm{phys}}=t_{\mathrm{slew}}(\theta_{\mathrm{FoR}})$ to tolerate worst-case cross-track slews. Angle-dependent dead time can replace it; inadequate resolution or classifier performance can exclude pitches from $\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{feas}}$.

\subsection{Lookahead Sensing Policy: Timing and Pitch}
\label{sec:lookahead_policy}

Each gap selects no action or an analytic initiation time plus one-dimensional pitch.

\paragraph{Latest feasible lookahead timing.}
\label{sec:timing}
Let $[t_{\mathrm{prev}}+t_s,t_{\mathrm{next}}]$ be a maximal idle gap.
Timing trades two opposing effects: a later observation sees farther into the future and can reveal more clear candidates, but a round trip that extends past $t_{\mathrm{next}}$ begins to displace the next scheduled image.
We admit at most one round trip and require it to finish by $t_{\mathrm{next}}$, preserving both anchors and eliminating that opportunity cost.
Within this feasible set, later observations weakly enlarge the revealed set, so the latest feasible start is
\begin{equation}
t^\star(\alpha) = t_{\mathrm{next}} - t_{\mathrm{man}}(\alpha),
\label{eq:optimal_timing}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\mathrm{man}}(\alpha) = 2(t_{\mathrm{slew}}(\alpha - \alpha_0) - t_s)$ is the round-trip maneuver time for pitch angle $\alpha$ and the \emph{actionable} observation window has length
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{act}}(t) = t + t_{\mathrm{horizon}} - t_{\mathrm{next}}.
\label{eq:actionable_window}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Latest Feasible Lookahead Timing]
\label{prop:timing}
Fix a feasible pitch angle $\alpha$ and restrict admissible lookahead maneuvers to round trips fully contained in the current schedule gap. Under the fixed gap-level baseline used by the policy, $t = t^\star(\alpha)$ maximizes the estimated net advantage $\widehat{\Delta J}$ over all admissible initiation times. At this time $\hat{J}^- = 0$, and the value gate is evaluated on the clipped detector support inside the fixed anchor interval.
\end{proposition}

A lookahead is feasible when $t_{\mathrm{next}}-t_{\mathrm{prev}}-t_s\geq t_{\mathrm{man}}(\alpha)$; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_proofs} proves the result.

\paragraph{Break-even pointing search.}
\label{sec:joint_opt}
Pitch trades access count against maneuver time: steeper views reveal more accesses but shorten actionability. The policy evaluates~\eqref{eq:g_break_alpha} on the feasible grid with fixed gap-level baseline, chooses $\alpha^\dagger$ from~\eqref{eq:break_even_margin} if $M(\alpha^\dagger)>0$, and otherwise does not look.

\subsection{Schedule Repair}
\label{sec:schedule_repair}

After a positive-value lookahead, receding-horizon re-optimization repairs only the affected segment~\citep{boerkoel_efficient_2021,nag_autonomous_2019,augenstein_optimal_2016}. The nearest scheduled tasks before and after the observed window anchor a local MILP over the pre-optimized schedule and alternate-access queue. Observed-cloudy accesses are removed; observed-clear and unobserved accesses remain, the latter at prior utility $pc_i$. The repaired segment is then spliced into the schedule.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:dt_loop} gives the break-even loop; experiments also test direct two-anchor scoring. Both assume correct, zero-latency classification within the lookahead FoV.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Dynamic tasking control loop}
\label{alg:dt_loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require initial schedule $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_0$, alternates queue $\mathcal{A}_{\text{alt}}$, angle grid $\mathcal{G}_\alpha$, cloud prior $p$
\State $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \gets \boldsymbol{\sigma}_0$
\For{each schedule gap $[t_{\mathrm{prev}}+t_s,\, t_{\mathrm{next}}]$ in $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$}
    \State $\alpha^\dagger \gets \arg\max_{\alpha \in \mathcal{G}_\alpha^{\mathrm{feas}}} M(\alpha)$ \Comment{break-even margin in~\eqref{eq:break_even_margin}}
    \If{$M(\alpha^\dagger) > 0$ \textbf{and} $t_{\mathrm{next}} - t_{\mathrm{prev}} - t_s \geq t_{\mathrm{man}}(\alpha^\dagger)$}
        \State at $t^\star(\alpha^\dagger) = t_{\mathrm{next}} - t_{\mathrm{man}}(\alpha^\dagger)$: slew to $\alpha^\dagger$, observe, slew back \Comment{Prop.~\ref{prop:timing}}
        \State update $\{X_i\}$ for accesses in the observed window
        \State $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \gets \textsc{MILP-Repair}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\, \mathcal{A}_{\text{alt}},\, \text{bounding accesses})$
    \EndIf
    \State execute scheduled imaging in $[t_{\mathrm{prev}}, t_{\mathrm{next}}]$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
